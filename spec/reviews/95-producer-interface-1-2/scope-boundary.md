# Scope-boundary review

## Summary

Targeted review of the whole scoped interface as one design: whether the
static/assessment split now holds in both directions, whether the producer has
annexed the evaluator's role, the consumer's role, or work owned elsewhere,
whether each obligation is allocated to exactly one named side, and whether
spec.md §2.1 and §2.2 describe the delivered boundary rather than the earlier
static-only one.

| FND-1860 | high | Cross-half. FR-123 annexes the evaluator's role in the same requirement that forbids it. Three Behavior clauses make the producer a reporter of truth dispositions: "The producer SHALL retain unchanged a decisive satisfied or violated disposition that a selected evaluator has already established", "The producer SHALL report the unavailable disposition, rather than a false or violated result, when an availability fact names an observation record inside the selected evaluator's exact admitted support set", and "retain the availability-incompleteness dimension ... independently of the disposition it reports". FR-123-AC-2 verifies exactly that behavior. Every other statement in the delivery allocates the disposition to the evaluator: FR-123's own "The producer SHALL NOT report a truth disposition of its own for any claim", its refusal of a document that does ("If a producer document reports a truth disposition of its own, then the producer SHALL refuse that document", AC-6), FR-123-CON-3 ("expose no operation that decides a claim's truth"), FR-108-AC-3 (the *evaluator* returns the unavailable or incomplete disposition), spec.md §2.1 ("availability facts the producer declares and whose truth consequence the selected evaluator decides"), §2.2 ("Evaluating a claim, deciding a truth disposition ... The producer declares availability and the selected evaluator decides what an unavailable fact prevents") and §6 ("no requirement in this range asserts a truth disposition"). As written the producer both reports and refuses a disposition, so FR-123 is unimplementable and the §6 status claim is false. Keep the producer obligation — emit the availability fact and the availability-incompleteness dimension — and restate the two disposition clauses and AC-2 as the evaluator-side consequence FR-108 owns, or delete them. | FR-123, FR-123-AC-2, FR-123-AC-6, FR-123-CON-3, FR-108-AC-3, spec.md §2.1, spec.md §2.2, spec.md §6 |

## Verdict

**CONDITIONAL** — both halves now exist and the split they were written to
protect holds in the direction the earlier review measured: nothing in the
assessment half makes an observation a prerequisite of static admission, the
binding runs one way only, and the consumer's role is respected throughout —
no consumer `SHALL`, no assigned table index, no defaulted member. The join
itself carries three high defects. The producer reports the truth dispositions
that FR-123's own prohibition, §2.2 and §6 all assign to the selected
evaluator, so FR-123 refuses what it requires. The canonical byte and digest
agreement that every assessment binding rests on is claimed by neither NFR:
NFR-036 excludes assessment documents and NFR-037 cites NFR-036 for them.
And FR-124 mandates export mappings for snapshot, window and observation-record
identities against a closed consumer vocabulary that has no such kind, a gap
only the consumer's repository could close and which nothing records as an
upstream change. Below those, the assessment half diverges from the static half
on the member set of two shared consumer records, reopens the native-clause-role
cardinality unit that D22 removed, tests a static-only export subset nobody
enumerates, requires a claim classification §2.2 excludes, and duplicates three
FR-112/FR-113 refusals — while the interface version, the document set, and the
closed-world universe are each obligations with no single named owner.
