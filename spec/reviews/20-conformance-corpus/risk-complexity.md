# Risk and complexity review

## Summary

Issue #20 builds a versioned conformance corpus under `conformance/` (17
construct-register families x 4 case classes, so at least 68 hand-authored
cases plus a mutation catalogue and a defect register), an independent
JSON-level oracle that re-decides the v1 cross-field rules and classifies
compatibility, a differential harness with an adapter registry and a dated
divergence register, a regenerated coverage account, and a published import
API. The intent — that the yardstick is not authored by the implementer — is
sound and is the only control that catches "every implementation wrong in the
same direction" (EC-038).

The largest technical risks are not in the oracle's rule logic; they are in the
comparison contract the harness rests on. The corpus compares adapters on an
ordered `code` plus `pointer` sequence, but `common.schema.json#/$defs/diagnostic`
seals its object and has no `pointer` member, the two merged readers report a
dotted `path`, and no published artifact declares a diagnostic-code registry at
all — so the corpus would mint the vocabulary it claims to derive. Schema-layer
negative cases cannot honour "exactly one diagnostic" under an IR schema built
from `oneOf`, `anyOf`, and `if/then`, and their instance pointers are
validator-specific across the three backend languages. Six of the mandated
negative cases and the package-version evolution case are not expressible at all
under a case model whose input is one `semantic-ir.schema.json`-valid document
plus a patch. `normalized` is defined as a fourth canonical form, distinct from
the contract's own `RFC8785-JCS-with-identity-sorted-sets-v1`.

The oracle is most likely to become a second bug rather than a check in its
compatibility classifier: `contracts-v1.md` makes an optional addition additive
only when every target and known consumer preserves, ignores, or surfaces it,
and makes stale or unknown consumer evidence block promotion, while FR-036
classifies a bare document pair unconditionally.

The largest volatility is the contract itself. `docs/semantic-data-system/contracts-v1.md`
is `status: provisional` with a `resolution_gate` on issue #9 "for its IR field
set", and every case pins a verbatim `quote` out of that prose while FR-035
makes any change to an existing `expected` block a major `corpusVersion` bump.

Safe to build first, in this order: the corpus schemas, the digest and
provenance gates, and the bases lifted from `fixtures/semantic/v1/positive/`
(FND-321); then the cross-field layer of the oracle seeded from the fifteen
codes already frozen in `fixtures/semantic/v1/negative/reader-cases.json`; then
the harness mechanics (registry, unmet rows, byte-identical report) against a
stub adapter. The schema-violation layer, the compatibility classifier, the
package- and lock-scoped negatives, and the `normalized` comparison should not
be tasked until FND-310..316 are decided.

## Top hazards

1. FND-310 / FND-311 - the comparison key. The corpus judges on a diagnostic member the contract's sealed diagnostic has no room for, and on codes no published artifact declares. Both must be settled before a single case is authored, because every case encodes them.
2. FND-314 - six of the eight mandated negatives and the package-version evolution case cannot be expressed as one `semantic-ir.schema.json`-valid document plus a patch. FR-038-AC-2 and FR-038-AC-8 fail by construction under FR-035's case model.
3. FND-316 - the compatibility classifier restates the contract more simply than the contract does (consumer evidence, targets, surfaces, most-restrictive aggregate all dropped), and `compatibility-report.schema.json` does not cover `1.1.0`. This is the requirement most likely to be wrong, and its wrongness would be invisible: four backends would be judged against it.
4. FND-312 / FND-315 - schema-layer error cascades and a corpus-local canonical form make two of the three comparison dimensions (`pointer` sequence, `normalized` bytes) unachievable across Rust, TypeScript, and Python validators.
5. FND-317 / FND-318 - the affordability pair. A provisional contract that will move its IR field set on issue #9, times ~90 to 110 cases each pinned to a verbatim prose quote and a digest row, is a corpus that will be maintained by regeneration - the failure US-008 names in its own risk section.

## Failure-domain gaps

No failure-domain review exists for issue #20; `spec/reviews/20-conformance-corpus/`
contains this review only, and the issue-#35 and issue-#34 failure-domain
reviews (SR-037, SR-028) predate FR-035..039. The edge-case rows carrying that
load today are EC-038..EC-045 in `spec/tests.md`. Of those, EC-039 (a case
regenerated from the implementation it judges) is the one FR-035's provenance
gates address directly; EC-042 (an `unavailable` backend read as a passing one)
is addressed by FR-037's unmet rows; and EC-044 (a divergence suppressed after
its defect is fixed) is the rule that FND-319 shows cannot hold under an
all-`unavailable` roster. EC-040 (a `1.0.0` document carrying an ungated `1.1.0`
node) and EC-041 (a construct detected by name matching) are open in the
contract itself, not in the corpus, and are the basis for FND-326 and FND-327.
EC-043 (locale-dependent diagnostic ordering) is covered by FR-036's byte-wise
pointer ordering but not by any cross-platform run (FND-328). A failure-domain
analysis over FR-035..039 should be run before `spec-to-plan`, specifically over
the identity and purity of the oracle's verdict object, which FND-310, FND-315,
and FND-325 each touch from a different side.
