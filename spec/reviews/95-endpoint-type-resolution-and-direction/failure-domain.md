## Summary

The batch was walked for the states it makes representable and does not govern:
identity confusion between a declared record and an endpoint's model type,
ambiguity when one type identity is exported more than once, resolution that
succeeds against the wrong correspondence's native artifact, the cascade FR-127's
new accounting drives into every existing fixture, and a consumer left with an
`undirected` relationship it must traverse. The two most serious are silent
rather than refusing: a type identity that collides with a record identity is
admitted resolving to a non-type export, and an endpoint's type can resolve
against a native artifact its own correspondence never names.

## Findings

| ID | Severity | Summary | Refs | Escape Cause |
| ------- | -------- | -------------------------------- | ------ | ------ |
| FND-850 | high | A type identity that collides with a declared record identity is admitted resolving to a non-type export, and FR-127-AC-6 blesses the behavior that produces it. `DeclaredExports::new` inserts component, endpoint and relationship identities as `Admits::Record(kind)` and then adds each endpoint's `typeIdentity` with `or_insert`, so a colliding identity keeps the record's exact-kind admission. The consequences the batch does not state: the identity is owed exactly one export mapping, its only admissible kind is the record kind, `is_declared_type` returns false so `ENDPOINT_TYPE_EXPORT_ABSENT` never fires, and the bundle is admitted with an endpoint whose model type resolves to a `component` export. FR-127's Description makes it normative that each named model type "resolves through exactly one export mapping to an exact native export kind", and CON-2 admits only the six type kinds for a declared model type — both are violated silently in exactly the case AC-6 describes. The correct disposition is a refusal naming the collision, not a precedence rule. | FR-127-AC-6, FR-127-CON-2, crates/baseline-producer/src/export.rs | wrong-requirement |
| FND-851 | high | An endpoint's model type can resolve against a native artifact its own correspondence does not name, and nothing in the batch binds the two. The existing fixture carries two correspondence records over two different native artifacts, with endpoints exported under the first and the `Order-successor` relationship exported under the second; `type/Order` is named by endpoints on both sides. One export mapping is owed for that type, it lives in whichever record authored it, and the cross-bound check in `validate_correspondence_set` only forbids the *same* identity appearing twice — it never requires an endpoint's type export to be carried by the same record that exports the endpoint. So a consumer holding artifact A resolves an endpoint of A to a type export of artifact B, with a kind and path drawn from B's table, and admission raises nothing. FR-127 states no same-correspondence rule, and FR-129 compounds it by describing the fixture as having one artifact while the fixture has two. | FR-127, FR-129, crates/baseline-producer/src/correspondence.rs | missing-requirement |
| FND-852 | high | FR-127's new accounting cascades into every existing fixture and the batch never says so. FR-116's rule that every declared identity is owed exactly one export mapping now ranges over endpoint model types as well as records, so `fixtures/baseline-1-2/static-bundle-a.json` — which declares `type/Order` and `type/Shipment` and carries export mappings for neither — refuses under `ENDPOINT_TYPE_EXPORT_ABSENT` the moment the change lands, as do the adverse and golden fixtures and every in-test bundle builder. No requirement in the batch owns that migration, no acceptance criterion measures the repaired fixture set, and FR-129 speaks only of the one fixture it repairs. A reader of FR-127 alone would size this as an additive resolution rule rather than a break of the entire admitted corpus. | FR-127, FR-116, fixtures/baseline-1-2/static-bundle-a.json | missing-requirement |
| FND-853 | high | FR-129's fixture cannot satisfy both "every export mapping drawn from the artifact's own export table" and FR-116's per-record accounting, because the native table has no kinds for the producer's own records. FR-116 obliges one export mapping per declared component, endpoint and relationship record, each carrying the matching `component`, `endpoint` or `relationship` kind; the real export table the batch selects against carries `field`, `scalar`, `object`, `operation`, `population`, `record` and `reference` and no producer-record kind at all. Either those eight record mappings are authored independently of the table — which CON-5 forbids in terms — or they are dropped, which FR-116 refuses. The batch states no partition between the record mappings (producer-authored) and the type mappings (drawn from the table), so the fixture as specified is unsatisfiable. | FR-129-CON-5, FR-116, FR-127 | wrong-requirement |
| FND-854 | high | A regenerated or renumbered export table is the failure this batch exists to prevent, and the batch disposes of it twice, incompatibly. FR-129's Behavior now requires the artifact's identity and revision to be pinned "so a regenerated artifact carrying a changed export table refuses"; its Dependencies section says a regenerated package with a changed export table "is not detected by this requirement". Neither statement is operationalized: the fixture's declared export kinds and paths are not themselves covered by any digest, so a regeneration that keeps identity and revision but renumbers or renames exports leaves the fixture declaring paths the artifact no longer has, and the only check that would notice — recomputing the artifact's digest — is satisfied by regenerating the fixture's digest alongside it. What would close it is a declared digest over the export table itself, or the artifact revision carried as a member the admission compares. | FR-129, FR-129-AC-1, US-018 | missing-requirement |
| FND-855 | medium | Two export mappings offered for one model type identity refuse under a code that names the wrong failure, and no requirement governs the case. FR-127 makes "exactly one export mapping" normative but states no behavior for a second; in `validate_correspondence_set` the duplicate falls into the `owners.insert` branch and raises `EXPORT_CROSS_BOUND` with the message "carries two export mappings under producer object X". A consumer triaging that refusal is told the export is bound across correspondences when in fact one record is ambiguous about a type. Two mappings agreeing in kind but differing in path, and two differing in kind, are also not distinguished — the first is a redundancy, the second a contradiction about what the type is. | FR-127, crates/baseline-producer/src/correspondence.rs | missing-requirement |
| FND-856 | medium | Resolution order and refusal order are unspecified where a bundle carries more than one defect. `DeclaredExports` is a `BTreeMap` and `validate_correspondence_set` returns on the first unmapped identity in lexicographic order, so a bundle missing both a record mapping and a type mapping surfaces whichever identity sorts first — `EXPORT_ABSENT` or `ENDPOINT_TYPE_EXPORT_ABSENT` depending on the spelling of the identities, not on the class of the defect. FR-127-AC-2 asks that the two refusals be distinct, which a test can satisfy while the other defect stays masked, and nothing in the batch states whether admission reports the first defect or all of them. Renaming an identity therefore changes which refusal a broken bundle produces. | FR-127-AC-2, FR-117, crates/baseline-producer/src/correspondence.rs | missing-requirement |
| FND-857 | medium | `undirected` leaves a consumer that must pick a traversal with no admitted move, and the batch names no obligation on either side. FR-128 gives `bidirectional` both traversals and `undirected` neither — so an undirected relationship is, at the producer seam, an edge no consumer may traverse in either direction, which is behaviourally indistinguishable from an edge that is not there. The requirement forbids the consumer manufacturing an orientation but states no alternative: not a refusal, not a required declaration that the consumer's operation is undefined for undirected edges, not a rule that an undirected relationship is presented to traversal-consuming operations at all. The naming makes it worse — in ordinary graph usage an undirected edge is traversable both ways, which is what this batch calls `bidirectional`, so a consumer reading the wire value without the interface table in hand will get the two exactly backwards. | FR-128, FR-128-AC-5, crates/baseline-producer/src/relationship.rs | missing-requirement |
| FND-858 | medium | The batch repairs one correspondence of the fixture's two and leaves the other synthetic. FR-129 speaks throughout of one artifact, one digest and one relationship, while `static-bundle-a.json` carries two correspondence records — the second over `order-profile-1-2` with digest `sha256:8888…8888` and placeholder closure entries `sha256:6666…6666`. FR-129's own prohibition on carrying "a native raw-byte digest composed of repeated placeholder fill in any fixture offered as evidence of type resolution" then applies to the fixture as a whole, so either the second record is repaired too (against which real artifact is unstated) or the fixture violates the requirement it is the evidence for. The native definition closure entries are in the same position: nothing in the batch requires their digests to be recomputed from anything. | FR-129, fixtures/baseline-1-2/static-bundle-a.json | missing-requirement |
| FND-859 | medium | The fixture must be both a self-relationship and not one, and the batch does not reconcile it. FR-129 prohibits satisfying the two-distinct-type-exports obligation with a self-relationship; the existing fixture's `Order-successor` relationship is a self-relationship on `type/Order`, and FR-128-AC-7 separately requires a self-relationship whose two ends name one type identity to be exercised under each of the four directions. The fixture therefore needs both shapes present, with only the non-self one carrying FR-129's obligation — which also means `type/Order` is resolved by endpoints belonging to both relationships and both correspondences, the condition FND-851 turns on. No requirement states which relationship carries which obligation. | FR-129, FR-128-AC-7, fixtures/baseline-1-2/static-bundle-a.json | missing-requirement |
| FND-860 | medium | The batch never says where the native bytes live, so every operational failure of reading them is ungoverned. CON-8 forbids vendoring the artifact, CON-7 forbids dependence on environment variables, working directory and network, and AC-1 requires the bytes to be hashed — leaving the location unstated and every failure mode with it: the artifact absent from the machine running admission, present but truncated, present at a stale revision, present but unreadable, or present in a developer's worktree and absent in CI. FR-129 defines a refusal only for a digest that does not match, not for bytes that cannot be obtained, so the likeliest real-world outcome — the fixture cannot be checked at all — has no stated disposition and would most naturally be implemented as a skipped test. | FR-129-AC-1, FR-129-CON-7, FR-129-CON-8 | missing-requirement |
| FND-861 | medium | FR-129 selects an artifact class whose export table the batch has not established exists. The revision re-points the fixture at "one native rule-model artifact that the native language admits as a native model" and away from the downstream compiled-protocol intake, while continuing to require every mapping to be drawn from "that artifact's own export table" with the kinds and paths "that table declares". The export table whose kinds the batch reasons about — including the `population` entry US-018-EX-3 depends on encountering — belongs to the emitted compiled-protocol package. If a rule-model artifact declares no export table, FR-127's resolution has nothing to resolve against here and the partition US-018-EX-3 wants exercised is never exercised; if it declares a different one, the batch's kind census does not describe it. | FR-129-CON-3, FR-129-AC-3, US-018-EX-3 | wrong-requirement |
| FND-862 | low | The native definition closure is required complete but never required consistent with the artifact. FR-129 obliges the producer to supply "the complete transitive native definition closure the fixture's correspondence requires", and `validate_selections` checks only that each declared required identity appears in the enumerated closure — a set the fixture itself authors on both sides. A closure that omits a definition the real artifact actually requires is admitted, because the requirement the closure is measured against is the fixture's own list rather than the artifact's. Against real bytes this is the same self-consistency defect the batch removes at the digest, left in place one member over. | FR-129, FR-116, crates/baseline-producer/src/correspondence.rs | correct-requirement-no-evidence |
| FND-863 | low | `bidirectional` is a single relationship admitting two traversals, and the batch does not say what a consumer that has already traversed it holds. FR-128 forbids treating `source-to-target` and `target-to-source` as two spellings of one relationship, and forbids permuting the members — but a consumer materializing traversals from a bidirectional edge produces two, both naming one relationship identity, and nothing states whether the pair is one edge observed twice or a duplicate the consumer must collapse. Identity of the traversal, as distinct from identity of the relationship, is unnamed in the batch. | FR-128, FR-128-AC-5 | missing-requirement |
| FND-864 | low | Every FR in the batch disclaims detection of the contract it depends on, in identical words: "a change on the consumer side is not detected by this requirement", now pinned at revision `5d76043b2af4308141817ce4a4055b03a0e5288f` in FR-129 and `72507f856457ba0922719bd5d9f5cadcce4058cd` in FR-127 and FR-128 — two different revisions of one external contract cited across one increment, with no statement of which is current or what happens when they diverge. The failure mode is the batch's own: the producer passes its suite against one pinned revision while the consumer has moved, which is the defect US-018 exists to stop, displaced from bytes to contract revisions. | FR-127, FR-128, FR-129 | missing-requirement |
